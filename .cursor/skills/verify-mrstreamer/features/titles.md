# Movies and series

## Sub-features

Movie and series libraries, search, quality/language filters, metadata and episode details, version choice, playback/resume, file tracks, online subtitles with saved timing, and marking episodes watched.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Open **Movies** or **Series** in the header. **All movies** and **All series** include every allowed title; other tabs filter and group the catalogue. The page's search is separate from global search. A poster opens details with Play/Resume, versions and Save. Series details show seasons and episodes. The compact Sort, Quality, Language and Verified menus filter the current grid or page search; global search stays unfiltered. Each menu lists every available choice with its current selection checked. Any clears that filter. Long menus scroll, and Escape returns focus to their trigger. With the menu closed, Escape leaves the open collection; application modifier shortcuts pass through the controls. Provider hint descriptions and locally verified-file counts are stated inside their menus, including zero verified files. Collection counts show the filtered and original totals beside the collection name. Sort remains local to the collection.

Online subtitles are configured in **Settings > General > Online subtitles**. During local movie or episode playback, **CC > Search subtitles** searches the enabled services. Choose a result there, or select a saved result under **Saved for this version**. Every subtitle control of a title is in that panel: **Timing** steps a file track, or edits the downloaded result's offset, with drift and the frame-rate conversions under **Drift and frame rate**; **Look** sets size, background and position; **Try the next result** sits with the search; **Settings** opens Settings > General on Online subtitles. **Forget downloaded subtitles** clears that file's downloaded results. The **Speed** button beside Sound sets the speed and nothing else. While a TV plays the title, CC is the small menu, with Subtitle timing and Subtitle look listed greyed.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

From desktop 0.0.16 the bundled tools decode VP8, VP9 and AV1 for title and offline conversion. `apps/desktop/scripts/verify-ffmpeg.ts <target>` checks the shipped features and every frame of the committed VP8 WebM, 10-bit VP9 MP4/WebM and 10-bit AV1 MKV/MP4 clips before and after H.264 conversion. A source build using system FFmpeg is not this proof. Native installed acceptance must additionally open, seek and play downloaded copies using the bundled tools, with decoded picture progress and no provider requests offline. Conversion outputs 8-bit SDR, without an HDR claim.

Opened provider details persist per saved source and accepted catalogue, with 24-hour freshness. TMDB descriptions and seasons persist per ID/language, refresh at 150 days, expire at 182 days and retry missing answers after one day. On an offline same-account restart, previously opened movie/series/season details remain readable with zero detail requests while valid. Refresh, changed credentials/import settings and removed titles must not restore fresh evidence for an old file. Failed renewal may retain listed display data; stale episodes cannot authorize playback, downloads or subtitle matching. `ondemand-details.test.ts` and `detail-cache.test.ts` cover restart, concurrency/cancellation, removal, clock correction, bounds and individual corrupt-record misses. Drive titles/downloads on the built app and inspect requests and retained screenshots separately. Explicit viewing-data erasure also securely removes shared title/season detail keys and timestamps, which otherwise reveal opened-title history. Ordinary source forgetting retains those shared descriptions.

Filtered page search counts every match while retaining only its best bounded page. Sparse matches beyond the first page remain findable, and the selected version must satisfy all file filters together. Prepared aliases always consult current metadata before reuse; expiry, missing answers, language changes and edited names must remove old spellings. `title-filters.test.ts` and the public core search corpus preserve totals, companion versions, exact-name ranking and account ownership. Measure first-process indexing separately from warm scans and query-cache hits.

Title delivery failures retry four times at 1/2/4/8 seconds. Brief returns, pauses, buffering and seeks do not restore the budget; 30 seconds of observed playback progress does. A new title or explicit Retry restores it. Local parser/MSE failures use media recovery, fetch/body failures use network recovery, and native MediaError code 2 is network. Preserve pause, position, tracks and one logical play through retries. `renderer/title-engine.test.ts` and `title-recovery.test.ts` cover the engine/controller contracts; native decoding/audio and real-provider acceptance remain separate.

Copied-video rejection in a local movie, episode or offline copy first tries useful sound conversion when the failing track is unknown, then H.264 video conversion once. A known rejected video codec can skip sound conversion. Startup and rejection after playback use the same finite session budget; paused recovery, seeks and track changes keep position, tracks and conversion. Network and typed upstream/tool failures do not spend that budget. `title-recovery.test.ts` and `title-engine.test.ts` exercise those controller/engine contracts, while `titles.test.ts` and `downloads.test.ts` serve high-depth copies and verify sticky conversion, clocks and one provider reader or zero offline requests. The synthetic `title-h264-10bit.mkv` and `title-hevc-12bit.mkv` derive from the existing two-track subtitle fixture using libx264/yuv420p10le and libx265/yuv420p12le, with stereo AAC and copied subtitles. Ordinary supported video still copies; receivers keep their existing H.264/AAC plans and cuts. Conversion retains existing SDR yuv420p behavior, without an HDR promise. Run `xvfb-run -a node apps/desktop/test/e2e/title-compatibility.ts <variant>` after building, with `MR_STREAMER_FFMPEG=/usr/bin/ffmpeg` for explicit Linux system-tool proof. Variants are `h264-10bit`, `hevc-12bit`, `h264-rejected-copy` and `h264-later-rejection`. The first two establish actual platform behavior. The rejected-copy variant keeps valid High 10 pictures but changes the AVC configuration's profile declaration to an invalid value, provoking real MSE type rejection; the later-rejection variant appends an impossible ISO box through the actual SourceBuffer after confirmed playback, provoking real browser MediaError code 3 twice to cover sound then video recovery while paused. Neither substitutes a fake engine or media error. Each drives Movies and Watch offline with fresh owned profiles, captures delivery headers, Chromium media diagnostics, decoded progress, pause/seek/audio/mini/fullscreen and one-reader/offline-request facts, retains screenshots/proof under `.local/verification/`, then tears down. Built-app rejection proof must record real MSE/decode refusal, actual response delivery and decoded frame/audio progress; service decoding alone does not prove browser playback. Native bundled and installed acceptance remains separate from Linux source-build proof.

Preconditions: built app with fresh fixture provider/TMDB. Run `pnpm verify:desktop titles`. Open Movies > All movies > the fixture poster whose title includes `Two sound tracks`, then close details and open Series > All series > a poster containing `Formats`. Require actual detail dialogs, Save buttons, at least two fake-TMDB detail requests and retained dialog screenshots. This baseline verifies browsing/details, not playback or every filter.

Catalogue updates during a first read must show current titles in Home, For you and All movies, even when the old IPC answer arrives after `ondemand.updated`. Cached posters stay visible during refresh. `renderer/ondemand-first-reads.test.ts` covers those rendered paths with held IPC answers; `renderer/episode-rows.test.ts` covers open details and seasons keeping their answers through the same event. The titles baseline does not force this timing. For built-app race proof, use the isolated Electron/CDP harness to hold the first list response at the IPC boundary, send the update event, release the old answer and inspect the resulting posters and read count.

For file subtitles, select a text track several minutes into an MKV, then reopen it with that track chosen and resume. Use a fake provider that delays range answers: upcoming text must show while recovery of earlier lines is still loading, and a long-lived line that began earlier must still arrive when recovery completes. "Subtitles loading" at the top right and "Loading" beside the chosen track in CC show only while no line the run has is due at the position, as Earlier/Later (G/H) time it: a line on screen silences both, a pause between lines while recovery runs brings them back, and a timing change while paused updates both at once. After the feed ends or breaks, "Unavailable" shows once its last line is over. An exact live text line still held in the feed's bounded memory is sent once, including across two quick skips with an intervening cancelled run; new text at the same timestamp still arrives. A new run fills lines missing from an old trimmed live buffer. Recovery failures for limit, unreadable and network keep known text; replacing the file clears it. The 35-second loading deadline keeps text and its feed active, ending only the loading state as unavailable, which is said only where no line is due; packet tracks still stop and clear at that deadline. PGS and DVB still wait for their recovered decoder state. Public service cases are in `apps/desktop/test/titles.test.ts`, with renderer cases in `apps/desktop/test/renderer/title-subtitles.test.ts`. MP4 text reads its own samples through the run and downloaded subtitles are whole files, so neither needs the MKV history scan.

A failed movie or episode, here or on a TV, says why in the middle of the picture, or in Next Up for a next episode that didn't start. Its cross (**Close message**, top right of the message) hides that message only: the phase stays failed, nothing claims the TV plays, the controls return and **Play** or Space tries again, and the next failure shows its own message. A lost TV's closed message stays closed while receivers are found or the list scans; reaching the TV again and losing it shows it again. **Play** on a lost TV that has gone from the list is refused before it connects, so the TV stays lost and "… didn't answer" shows at the top right for a few seconds, each retry its own and closed with its own cross. Tab to the cross and Enter or Space closes it without starting anything, and focus moves to Play. The mini player's failure word and "Subtitles unavailable" close the same way. Renderer cases are in `next-episode.test.ts`, `receiver.test.ts` and `title-subtitles.test.ts`; in the built app, fail an open with the fake provider, close the message, require the controls and a retry request.

For movie playback, seeking, release of provider connections and subtitles use `apps/desktop/test/e2e/packaged-app.ts`. That installed-package smoke also replaces a previously unopened MP4 movie with a bounded runtime-generated VP9/AAC MP4 from committed fixtures, then opens it through Movies and requires decoded picture/audio and a ten-second seek. The provider changes the actual bytes and file identity; no probe or capability state is seeded. For episode menus, numbering and watched-state behavior use `episode-marks.ts`. Inspect `TitlesPage.tsx` and the current filter labels before driving Quality/Language, and compare the visible matching poster/count and selected version. Filtering on Verified requires a file already observed by real playback; do not seed observations by writing internal state.

The titles baseline does not configure online subtitle services. For online proof, use the existing Electron/CDP harness in `apps/desktop/test/e2e/app.ts` with fixture service responses at the external API/download boundary. Require zero service requests after configuration or opening CC, a request after Search, and downloaded cues over a picture that keeps playing. Edit timing, switch cached results with no new download, then restart the app and require that the same exact file restores its text and correction without a service request. Another file version must start without that correction. Choosing Off or a file track during restore must leave the saved result available. Choosing Off, a file track or a saved result during a download, or pressing C, must cancel that download and ignore its late success or failure. Choose Off or a file track, leave and reopen the same file: the saved result must stay listed and not show, the subtitle language in `preferences.get` must be what it was before that choice, and choosing the result must show it again with no service request and survive the next reopen. The same choice with nothing saved held, as during a first download, is remembered as the language for other titles. An explicit download must restore after a restart even with the subtitle language Off. Change the fixture file's strong ETag and read the session's playback address once more: the downloaded cue and its saved row must leave while the picture and the file's own tracks go on, and Search must be unavailable until the title is opened again. A provider file small enough to be held whole is not read again by a skip, so the skip alone does not establish a replacement.

## Artwork and sheet geometry

`PosterContent` in `components/TitleArt.tsx` owns the 2:3 frame and the name/caption block. Related rows and Home assign widths; tiles fill them. `CollectionGrid` uses the same aspect ratio and caption allowance for virtual row height. Check loaded pictures of different intrinsic dimensions, missing/failed pictures and long names at narrow/wide sizes and after zoom; labels and frames must stay inside their assigned slots while horizontal scrolling and keyboard opening work.

`Sheet` owns the rounded outer clip; its inner viewport owns vertical scrolling and fills tall sheets. Verify tall movie/series details, short unavailable Watchlist sheets and the Settings playlist/guide mapping sheets with few or no search results. Map footers stay at the bottom of tall sheets. Check Close, Escape, outside click, initial/returned focus and receiver-bar spacing. The scrollbar must remain inside the top corner. Genre/service tile pages leave space above the first row for its hover/focus border; inspect that edge and open a collection from the tile.

## Gotchas

Category compatibility uses `docs/user/category-compatibility.md`. Xtream movie and series memberships share the Live adapter's conversion, with unchanged raw title IDs. Core playlist-import cases prove inherited `#EXTGRP` groups follow explicit Movie/Series picks and retain version IDs when blocks reorder. These are public contracts; title-version playback and installed restart acceptance still require the corresponding app path.

A provider that ignores byte ranges can resume and skip by reading its whole answer forward to the requested byte position. Verify both local playback and a receiver segment away from zero against ranged playback, including decoded pictures, start timestamps, bytes sent and one provider connection. `titles.test.ts` and `receiver.test.ts` cover those service paths. Such a provider is never asked for a subtitle track's past: the lines before a position come only from what a run from the start kept of the same file, so after a whole run a skip shows the line on screen with no provider request, while before one, after a replaced file, or with answers that carry no strong validator (a strong ETag, or a Last-Modified at least a second older than the answer) or don't say how long the file is, it is `unreadable`. Keyframe-index reads still refuse it. Correct 206 answers whose headers do not establish the range still pass through as before; verify their exact bytes. Local startup waits while discarded prefix bytes advance, then gives the picture a full 30 seconds after the final byte. A drop stalled for 30 seconds fails as before; leaving aborts the wait immediately. Main sends `playback.readingAhead` at most once every 3 seconds only as dropping advances. The starting title engine follows matching session notices for its existing 45-second no-picture and 30-second unpaused-start watchdogs. With no notices it fails as before, and ranged providers send none. Verify an advancing slowed far resume lasting more than 30 seconds through a moving picture, plus a stalled answer that fails. Service cases in `titles.test.ts` use an injected startup deadline; `renderer/title-reading-ahead.test.ts` uses the real player and engine with a held response and browser media stand-ins. Receivers retain the 30-second segment deadline, so a large or slow far segment can return 504 and a retry can read from zero again.

TMDB enrichment changes displayed names; fixture poster titles still include provider names. A populated grid is not proof of playback, filters or version choice. Adult content needs its explicit Settings switch. Some verified series tracks only become filterable after reopening their details; check current user docs before treating absence as a defect.

Downloaded text and timing belong to the exact file and play locally. Receivers keep supported file tracks. Removing a subscription retains its saved subtitle data unless the viewer also deletes its viewing data. After a catalogue refresh, or once 200 other titles' details were read, the next subtitle command of a playing episode asks the provider for its series' details once: the same listing keeps the saved result, timing and search, another listing ends them until the title is opened again, and no answer refuses them until the provider answers. Off or a file track over the saved result is kept for the file playback still holds, with or without that answer. A failed save there is a finding, not proof.

Main sends the service requests with its own `fetch`, so a window-side network stub never sees them. Start the app with `--inspect-brk`, replace `globalThis.fetch` for the service hosts while main is paused, and start its workers without that flag. The player ignores its keys while the CC panel or a menu is open, and closing the panel cancels a pending request. Escape closes the panel from anywhere, also after a pointer pick left focus on the page: pick a result with the pointer, then press Escape and require the panel gone. To press C during a download, start one with **Try the next result** in the panel, then close it. A saved result shows again when its file reopens, unless Off or a file track was the last choice there. C then turns on the file's first own track, or the saved result when the file has none.

To have main save a download before the window reads the reply, hold the fixture's download, schedule its release from main, then in one window evaluation spin for longer than that and click the choice at the end: the click is handled before the queued reply. Read main's rows through a read-only database connection. Open **CC** with real key presses (Enter on the button): focus must land on the chosen row, Tab and Down walk the panel in reading order through Forget, Reset, the offset steps and the look, and the drift ratio must be what it was after a step. Enter needs its character sent with the key down, or the button is not activated. At 960 by 600 the panel scrolls inside itself.
