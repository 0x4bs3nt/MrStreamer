# Movies and series

## Sub-features

Movie and series libraries, search, quality/language filters, metadata and episode details, version choice, playback/resume, file tracks, online subtitles with saved timing, and marking episodes watched.

Opening a title or offline copy claims the shared media element and ends the previous live playback. Explicit live playback closes the title and cancels even a countdown held by Settings; preview and stale live retries cannot replace it. Same-title recovery, next episode, paused engine release and receiver following retain title ownership until close. Public handoff/adoption/late-preview cases are in `renderer/playback-admission.test.ts`; held-countdown handoff is in `renderer/next-episode.test.ts`. Built-app and native handoff checks follow the Live TV feature entry, alongside downloads' provider-preemption and offline paths.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Open **Movies** or **Series** in the header. **All movies** and **All series** include every allowed title; other tabs filter and group the catalogue. The page's search is separate from global search. A poster opens details with Play/Resume, versions and Save. Series details show seasons and episodes. The compact Sort, Quality, Language and Verified menus filter the current grid or page search; global search stays unfiltered. Each menu lists every available choice with its current selection checked. Any clears that filter. Long menus scroll, and Escape returns focus to their trigger. With the menu closed, Escape leaves the open collection; application modifier shortcuts pass through the controls. Provider hint descriptions and locally verified-file counts are stated inside their menus, including zero verified files. Collection counts show the filtered and original totals beside the collection name. Sort remains local to the collection.

Details show **Also in your subscriptions**, related titles from every saved subscription's loaded lists, never titles for adults; an adult title's own row is empty. A related title opens its details in place, and **Back** in the top bar the sheet keeps above its backdrop returns through those it was opened from, at most eight, with the season picked, the scroll and the keyboard on the related title opened. With none left, Back returns to the page, as Close and Escape do at once; the keyboard then returns to the control that opened the first details, when it is still on the page. A title opened from a page, search or the player starts a new way back. Playing a title or opening Settings over the details keeps it. Removing a subscription takes its titles out of it and closes details of its own; hiding titles for adults and removing the last subscription close it all. Back asks the lists first: a title they have since marked for adults while Settings hides them is passed over and never shows. Each visit is a sheet of its own, so Back to an earlier visit of the same title restores it at once. Search opened over details, from their bar or with ⌘K or Ctrl K, gives the keyboard back to what had it. The trail lives in `app/ui-store.ts` (`openRelated`, `backDetails`, `closeDetails`) for the session only.

Online subtitles are configured in **Settings > Subtitles > Online search**. During local movie or episode playback, **CC > Search subtitles** searches the enabled services. Choose a result there, or select a saved result under **Saved for this version**. Every subtitle control of a title is in that panel: **Timing** steps a file track, or edits the downloaded result's offset, with drift and the frame-rate conversions under **Drift and frame rate**; **Look** sets size, background and position; **Try the next result** sits with the search; **Settings** opens Settings > Subtitles on Online search. **Forget downloaded subtitles** clears that file's downloaded results. The **Speed** button beside Sound sets the speed and nothing else. While a TV plays the title, CC is the small menu, with Subtitle timing and Subtitle look listed greyed.

Next-episode autoplay and its exact whole-second delay are separate controls in **Settings > Playback**. The optional `nextEpisodeDelaySeconds` accepts 0 through 60, with 10 as the default and **Instant** at 0. Each ended episode snapshots its delay. Settings holds even Instant, then rechecks the final saved autoplay policy on release. Final Off or Cancel leaves manual **Next episode**, N and media-next available. An off/on edit while held resumes the saved remainder; delay edits apply to the next countdown. A failed policy read leaves manual Next. Unsupported stored delay values use 10 effectively while the raw leaf survives unrelated saves and original-source removal.

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop next-episode` after building for actual media-ended paths at 0, custom 37 and 60. It selects the two-season Dutch fixture through Versions and reaches the Position range with Tab before seeking with End. It captures the held Instant and countdowns, checks hold/off/on/cancel/manual Next, renders the numeric row, Instant and active Next Up in all five locales, and records decoded media and provider file request counts. The first fake episode serves the existing H.264/AAC clip to isolate sequencing from EAC3 compatibility. File requests include probes/ranges and are not logical session counts. Exact timing, single-open ordering, obsolete reads, receiver commands and an offline copy's independence from the viewing record are public-controller cases in `renderer/next-episode.test.ts` and `renderer/receiver.test.ts`; a downloaded episode going on to the next episode's copy, with the same delay, hold, Cancel and autoplay rules, is in `renderer/copy-next.test.ts` and the [Downloads entry](downloads.md); persistence and older-reader round trips are in `preferences.test.ts`. This scenario does not establish native installed, audible-output or receiver-hardware acceptance.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

Require per-window delivery headers, decoded picture/audio progress and no served provider reads after fixture shutdown; an unavailable required rejection branch remains unresolved. Native acceptance needs an actual run. Conversion outputs 8-bit SDR, without an HDR claim.

Opened provider details persist per saved source and accepted catalogue, with 24-hour freshness. TMDB descriptions and seasons persist per ID/language, refresh at 150 days, expire at 182 days and retry missing answers after one day. On an offline same-account restart, previously opened movie/series/season details remain readable with zero detail requests while valid. ## Poster-grid focus

`Grid` in `features/titles/CollectionGrid.tsx` is the one owner of keys, selection and focus for collections, a kind's search and the Watchlist. The keys move a selection that DOM focus follows; the selected poster is the grid's one Tab stop and the next Tab leaves the grid. Its button is named by title and year, with the subscription where the caption names one, inside a list item with `aria-posinset` and `aria-setsize`. Enter on it opens it once; Space is the button's own click. The selection keeps to a title by `Title.key` while pages arrive, the list is read again or the width and text size change, and its row stays drawn when scrolled away. A refresh reads the loaded pages again one at a time: a title they lack keeps its key, with no ring or focus and no Enter, until every page answered, and only then counts as gone, when what stands at its position takes its place. A title that moved past the loaded pages isn't looked for beyond them. A press with the pointer opens a poster and makes it the selection without showing the ring. Closing details puts the focus back on the poster that opened them, by key and in view, when the grid had the focus; it runs after the sheet's own return of focus, which can name a poster an earlier popup recorded. Down or Enter in the kind's search hands the focus to the selected result once the results are the query's, unless the field has the focus again first. Focus the viewer gives another control cancels what the grid still meant to focus: a key's selection whose page is on its way, or the place of a removed tile. Keys inside dialogs, menus, fields and the filter bar never reach the grid. Renderer cases in `renderer/poster-grid.test.ts` cover pages answering in either order, a second refresh shortening the old page while the selected title is already loaded at its new position, details closing before or after, and the focus moved elsewhere meanwhile; the maintained scenario holds no IPC answers, so a built-app check of those needs a held real answer in an owned instance.

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop poster-grid` after building. With 400 fixture movies it tabs into Movies > All movies and out, moves with the arrows, Page Down across the first page's end, End and Home, and requires focus, ring, position, set size, one Tab stop and the visible poster to agree at each step, plus the focused accessibility node's role, name and list item. Enter opens the focused poster and Escape returns to it; a pointer press opens another and Escape returns there. At 960x600, and then with a 21 px root size standing in for larger text as `settings` does, the same title keeps the focus in view. In search, Down before the results arrive focuses the first result, Escape from its details returns to it, and typing again keeps the focus in the field. In the Watchlist, four titles saved through details give one stop for the selected poster and one for its cross; a removal with the keyboard leaves the focus on the cross of the tile that took its place, and of the one before it after the last. Renderer cases are in `renderer/poster-grid.test.ts`. Linux CDP and Xvfb establish DOM focus and Chromium's accessibility tree only: what VoiceOver and NVDA announce, native text size and zoom remain native release checks.

## Gotchas

Category compatibility uses `docs/user/category-compatibility.md`. Xtream movie and series memberships share the Live adapter's conversion, with unchanged raw title IDs. Core playlist-import cases prove inherited `#EXTGRP` groups follow explicit Movie/Series picks and retain version IDs when blocks reorder. These are public contracts; title-version playback and installed restart acceptance still require the corresponding app path.

A playlist film's headers go whole only to its file's origin: after a redirect elsewhere, a Referer holding the link's token goes cut to its origin, for playback, a receiver and downloads alike. The file's own token-named query values don't follow an https redirect to http where the login rule recognizes them, unlike a Live stream's; its other query values count only as whole parts. `playlist-credentials.test.ts` checks probing and a download transfer against a loopback http server; `playlist-import.test.ts` keeps the exact-file and Live redirect policies.

A provider that ignores byte ranges can resume and skip by reading its whole answer forward to the requested byte position. Verify both local playback and a receiver segment away from zero against ranged playback, including decoded pictures, start timestamps, bytes sent and one provider connection. `titles.test.ts` and `receiver.test.ts` cover those service paths. Such a provider is never asked for a subtitle track's past: the lines before a position come only from what a run from the start kept of the same file, so after a whole run a skip shows the line on screen with no provider request, while before one, after a replaced file, or with answers that carry no strong validator (a strong ETag, or a Last-Modified at least a second older than the answer) or don't say how long the file is, it is `unreadable`. Keyframe-index reads still refuse it. Correct 206 answers whose headers do not establish the range still pass through as before; verify their exact bytes. Local startup waits while discarded prefix bytes advance, then gives the picture a full 30 seconds after the final byte. A drop stalled for 30 seconds fails as before; leaving aborts the wait immediately. Main sends `playback.readingAhead` at most once every 3 seconds only as dropping advances. The starting title engine follows matching session notices for its existing 45-second no-picture and 30-second unpaused-start watchdogs. With no notices it fails as before, and ranged providers send none. Verify an advancing slowed far resume lasting more than 30 seconds through a moving picture, plus a stalled answer that fails. Service cases in `titles.test.ts` use an injected startup deadline; `renderer/title-reading-ahead.test.ts` uses the real player and engine with a held response and browser media stand-ins. Receivers retain the 30-second segment deadline, so a large or slow far segment can return 504 and a retry can read from zero again.

TMDB enrichment changes displayed names; fixture poster titles still include provider names. A populated grid is not proof of playback, filters or version choice. Adult content needs its explicit Settings switch. Once it is off, Movies and Series offer no Adults tab or its titles, including at first paint after the switch and from an answer read while it was on; `renderer/adult-visibility.test.ts` holds those answers. In the built app, turn it on, read Movies > Adults, go Home, turn it off and open Movies with a DOM observer. Some verified series tracks only become filterable after reopening their details; check current user docs before treating absence as a defect.

Downloaded text and timing belong to the exact file and play locally. Receivers keep supported file tracks. Removing a subscription retains its saved subtitle data unless the viewer also deletes its viewing data. After a catalogue refresh, or once 200 other titles' details were read, the next subtitle command of a playing episode asks the provider for its series' details once: the same listing keeps the saved result, timing and search, another listing ends them until the title is opened again, and no answer refuses them until the provider answers. Off or a file track over the saved result is kept for the file playback still holds, with or without that answer. A failed save there is a finding, not proof.

Main sends the service requests with its own `fetch`, so a window-side network stub never sees them. Start the app with `--inspect-brk`, replace `globalThis.fetch` for the service hosts while main is paused, and start its workers without that flag. The player ignores its keys while the CC panel or a menu is open, and closing the panel cancels a pending request. Escape closes the panel from anywhere, also after a pointer pick left focus on the page: pick a result with the pointer, then press Escape and require the panel gone. To press C during a download, start one with **Try the next result** in the panel, then close it. A saved result shows again when its file reopens, unless Off or a file track was the last choice there. C then turns on the file's first own track, or the saved result when the file has none.

To have main save a download before the window reads the reply, hold the fixture's download, schedule its release from main, then in one window evaluation spin for longer than that and click the choice at the end: the click is handled before the queued reply. Read main's rows through a read-only database connection. Open **CC** with real key presses (Enter on the button): focus must land on the chosen row, Tab and Down walk the panel in reading order through Forget, Reset, the offset steps and the look, and the drift ratio must be what it was after a step. Enter needs its character sent with the key down, or the button is not activated. At 960 by 600 the panel scrolls inside itself.
