# Downloads

## Sub-features

Download a movie, a whole series or a single episode from its details, a persistent queue with one transfer at a time, pausing and resuming one download or the whole queue, moving waiting downloads up or down, the top bar's notice of how it stands, the Downloads page with queue and copies, a series' copies together by season, offline playback of a copy in the title player and going on to the next episode's copy, playback of the same subscription taking the connection, cancel, retry, delete, interrupted and replaced files, and copies kept after their subscription is removed.

On iOS, a transfer that starts from zero again says **started again**. That state also covers unusable or absent resume data; it does not establish that the provider changed the file. Check through ordinary Downloads Retry with the same fixture bytes and ETag, then compare the final media hash. An uninterrupted transfer must keep its normal progress text after saving progress. Native resume requires its own native attempt and wire-range binding.

## How to get to it (user POV)

A movie's details have **Download** beside Play and Save; a series' **Download** menu previews all available seasons, including Specials, its selected Play version and eligible/already-downloaded/already-queued counts. Confirm **Download … episodes** to append one file per logical episode in `seriesEpisodeOrder`, using the same `episodeFileVersion` rule as the row, with a resumed file only while listed. Complete, transferring, waiting and queued files are skipped; failed files use the existing Retry. Partial Add failures retain successful additions and name failed episodes with Retry. Changing the selected episode files, including marking a resumed episode watched or refreshing the default language, hides the old result and Retry. A late answer cannot restore that obsolete feedback; copies already queued remain in Downloads. The menu aggregates the existing statuses and offers Retry failed, Cancel the rest and Open Downloads. A version switch or unmount stops further additions after any submitted call finishes. Each episode row has a fixed icon target (`Download S1 E2`), status first in the facts line and a 3px progress bar under its still. Its cancel, retry, missing and offline icons keep the overflow control aligned. Movie and series pills reserve their widest state label in the current language, at least 200px, so Save keeps its place in every state. The control follows the download: Queued, a percentage, Waiting for playback, an error with Retry, then **Watch offline** (a movie's arrow beside it holds **Delete download**; an episode's reads **Downloaded**). **Downloads**, the last page in the top bar, lists the queue, then the copies on this computer with **Watch offline** and **Delete** (pressed twice). The queue goes in its order: the transfer first with **Pause** and **Cancel**, then the downloads waiting their turn, each with Up and Down (`Move <name> up`/`down`, one place among the waiting, off at either end), **Pause** or **Resume** and **Cancel**, then the failed ones with **Retry** and **Delete**. A paused one reads **Paused · <bytes> of <size>** over its still bar and keeps its place until resumed, also through a restart and playback; one whose subscription plays reads **Waiting while <subscription> plays**. The heading has **Pause all** while anything can be paused, else **Resume all** while something is. A pause, resume or move that can't be saved says **Couldn't save. Try again.** on its row, or under the heading, with its control left to press again. A row's button keeps the keyboard wherever main's list moves the row, as React gives it back while drawing the move, and where a move turns its arrow off at an end the row's other arrow takes it in that same drawing. Nothing waits for main's answer, so lists and answers may come in any order; a keyboard the viewer took to another row, or that went with a row cancelled meanwhile, is left alone. A movie's details then read **Paused · Resume**, an episode's icon resumes it (`Resume download of S1 E2`) and its facts say **Paused**; the series menu counts it as already queued, and Cancel the rest ends it. Below the width where the visible page names fit it is the download icon beside Search, with the same name. While a download transfers, waits or is queued, the top bar's notice beside Search says so on every page but Downloads with its name in the bar: the arrow and a floored percentage, the bytes so far when the size is unknown (Starting before the first), **Waiting**, **Queued** or, once only paused ones are left, **Paused**, muted; **Download stopped** once a failure of a saved subscription is all that is left. Its name is "Downloads, <what it says>". Where the bar folds, it is the folded Downloads button, marked on its page. Where the other pages' names, or Back, have no room beside its words, it keeps its arrow alone and says them on hover and focus. Pressing it, with the pointer, Enter or Space, opens Downloads; over a playing title it leaves the title as Back does first, and over Watch or a title the player's keys leave Enter and Space on it to the button. With no subscription saved, Connect shows **Downloads** top right while a copy exists; that page has only Downloads and **Add subscription**.

On this computer, two or more copies of episodes this build or a later one queued from the same series version of the same account go together where the newest of them is: a row with the series' poster and name, the subscription (or **Subscription removed, copy kept**), how many episodes and seasons are here and their size, and an arrow (`aria-expanded`, named after the series) that folds the episodes away in place. Under it each **Season N** or **Specials** lists its copies in the series' own order, each by its number and name with its length, **Watched** once finished, progress and time left once started, and **Watch offline** (`Watch S1 E2 offline`) and **Delete**. An episode the series lists between two copies with no copy here says `S2 E3 isn't on this computer`, or `S2 E3 to S2 E6 aren't on this computer` for a run. The series goes by what it listed as its latest download was queued (`DownloadList.series`), numbered seasons first and specials apart, or a mapped playlist's source order; copies of episodes it no longer lists come last. A series' only copy, a movie and an episode an earlier build queued stay single rows. Copies go together by series version, account and TMDB id, under a key made up for each app run that says nothing of the account; names never join them.

At the end of a copy whose series lists a next episode, the end screen counts down to that episode's copy with the **Next episode** setting and delay: `Finished S1 E2 · Offline`, `S1 E3 · <name>`, `<length> · Plays in <n>`, **Play now** and **Cancel**; once cancelled or with autoplay off it offers **Next episode** and **Downloads**, and N, media-next and the controls' **Next episode** play it too. Settings holds the countdown as online. The next copy opens only once main kept this copy's progress and main's list, read again, still has that copy complete: otherwise the end stays on this copy. A save main refuses, as while another copy of the app holds the database, keeps this copy where it is, playing or at its end, shows `Couldn't save. Try again.` and stops the countdown; nothing goes on by itself, and Next, N or the end's **Next episode** tries again. A next episode with no copy, or whose file went, says `S1 E3 isn't on this computer` and `Download it to go on.`; one still downloading says `S2 E5 is still downloading` with its status line, one failed `S2 E5 didn't download`; after the series' last episode it says `That was the last episode`; each offers **Downloads** and **Back**. Nothing skips to a later copy. A copy without a series ends as before, with **Play again**.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

Long-series progress checks use 520 episodes in 20 seasons, 2,000 unrelated complete copies and one advancing transfer. Unrelated progress must leave the selected episode statuses and details view unchanged; progress for that exact subscription/file must update its still bar, facts and action. Batch add/cancel/retry reads current queue snapshots after awaited commands, including a new completion or removed missing copy. `renderer/series-download.test.ts`, `downloads.test.ts` and `episode-rows.test.ts` preserve those contracts. `download-performance.probe.ts` is a separate React/happy-dom diagnostic, not native paint evidence.

## Gotchas

For download-progress profiling, run `node apps/desktop/test/e2e/download-performance.ts --app-dir <frozen app directory> --executable <Electron> --revision <sha> --output <new evidence directory>` serially for each build. It seeds 2,000 completed records while the app is closed, then drives a 520-episode series while a real transfer advances. Retain all 30 content/scroll samples, main snapshots, CPU profiles, traces and matching-row positive control. Task/script time and a subsequent animation-frame callback do not prove physical paint latency or retained-heap savings. The functional Downloads driver waits for a pointer-reachable control after Search closes; a mounted control can still be behind its closing overlay.

Fixture names come back from TMDB as "Original 100000" and similar: the scenario finds rows by the download id main lists, never by provider names. The 20-second fixture is all credits by the 30-second rule, so resume is proved on the 150-second one. The first track of the long-subtitles fixture is pictures, drawn from a metadata track: text cues need its French track. Home's muted preview is playback too, so a download of that subscription waits while Home shows it. The proof uses fixtures and a development build on Linux; disk-full and folder failures are injected at the disk boundary in the service tests, not provoked on a real disk. Native macOS and Windows installs, a real provider and real disk exhaustion need their own checks. The fixtures say each file's size, so the notice's bytes and Starting are covered by the renderer tests only. Layouts below 960 CSS px are CDP viewport emulation, standing in for a zoomed window. The update notice and the TMDB ring beside the notice are not shown in this scenario; the bar folds by what it measures, so they count as any other width there, and the renderer test dismisses Update with its layout supplied. The arrow tries its words again when the pages' room grows, the words change or the interface language does, measured before the bar is drawn. On Windows, the [installed upgrade check](../../../../docs/contributing/testing.md#installed-upgrade) downloads two movies and the selected S1 E2 file on the actual stable release before installing the candidate over it. Stable plays the copies, observes saved subtitle timing and cues, and leaves nonzero local movie progress. Candidate entry requires the same three completed IDs, saved details, artwork and progress before any playback, with no replacement download; copied playback must resume stable's saved position. The same candidate then restarts, retains those copies, removes the subscription and plays them with the fixture services stopped and the app, bundled ffmpeg and ffprobe proven blocked from the network. The restart is not another upgrade. Acceptance requires the actual Windows run; stage-contract stand-ins do not prove the installer transition or installed codec recovery.

The fake provider holds a file once per request, at its byte: a download resumed from that byte isn't held again and finishes at once. The queue scenario therefore holds a download further on before playback ends and before each start, or it would finish between two reads. A pause answers only once main has written where the transfer stopped, and main's list can arrive before or after that answer.

The folded-page check probes 760 down to 520 CSS px in 20 px steps and records `notice.foldedWidth`, requiring the actual folded Downloads control and current-page marker before exercising its pointer/keyboard paths. Header copy changes the width where folding starts; 760 px alone may still fit all page names. The existing 960/760 layout checks remain required separately.

The Back/notice fold probe checks 960 down to 320 CSS px in 20 px steps, stopping at the first arrow-only notice. System fonts and native window-control insets change that boundary; text that fits at 520 px is valid. Every probed width must still fit Back and all controls, the shorter percentage must regain its words at the same width, and Settings must fold there too. The separate 520 px Settings check still requires an unclipped bar.
