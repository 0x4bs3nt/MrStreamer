# Live TV

## Home

Home plays the channel you watched last, muted, in a compact preview with what's on it now: the programme, how long it has left and what comes next. **Watch** opens it full window with sound, on the same stream, so it doesn't start again. The speaker button turns the sound on without leaving Home.

Below the preview, Home starts with **Continue watching**, local **Recommendations** and **Watchlist**, then favourite and recent channels, new movies, new series and your current category. Rows with nothing to show stay out. **All** opens a full list, and **All channels** opens every channel.

**Watchlist** in the top navigation bar opens all saved titles while any are visible under your adult-content setting, even when its Home row is hidden. The entry hides when there are none. **Settings > Home** lists every row, including empty ones. Use the checkboxes to show or hide rows and the up/down buttons to reorder them. Tab reaches each control; Enter activates a move and Space changes a checkbox. Changes save immediately and survive restart. **Reset to defaults** restores the order above and shows every row.

Recommendations use up to three available titles from a bounded recent local viewing record and the catalogue already on your computer. Each suggestion names the title you watched and its matching genre, category or name. Without viewing history, Home keeps its ordinary catalogue and channel rows. Titles for adults never appear on Home, even with **For adults** on.

Until you've watched something, the backdrop shows a channel without playing it.

Your subscription may allow a single connection. Home then uses it as soon as it opens, so if another device is already watching, the backdrop stays still instead of taking the connection over. With [several subscriptions](subscriptions.md), Home plays the channel you watched last, whichever it is from. Minimising Mr. Streamer, hiding it behind other windows, or going to Movies or Series stops the muted backdrop; it starts again when you come back.

## The guide

**Live TV** keeps Favourites, Recently watched and All channels, then [your own lists](#your-lists), beside nine fixed topics: News, Sports, Kids, Movies, Entertainment, Music, Documentary, Regional and Other. Topics span [every subscription you added](subscriptions.md#everything-together). They describe the provider's category labels, never a guessed channel identity. A channel can appear in several content topics. Regional is for recognized place-only category labels; uncertain labels stay in Other. All channels and **Provider lists** always remain available.

Country, Language and Source filter the current selection. Several choices within one filter match any of them; different filters apply together. Each menu counts its alternatives under the other active filters. Unknown country or language stays reachable with Any and through Provider lists. **Reset filters** clears the session filters. F opens Country; Tab reaches the other filters, and the menus take Up, Down, Enter, Space and Escape. Watch shows the same active filters and Reset.

**Provider lists** opens your provider's countries and categories in their original order. Open a country to see its categories; Left or Backspace returns. **Find** searches those country and category names across the catalogue, separately from channel and programme search. Duplicate category names include source context.

The last topic, provider category or custom list is remembered globally. All channels clears that choice, so Home and restart stay on All. Filters reset on a different base selection and at startup, and stay when returning from Watch to the same selection. Favourites and Recently watched are never remembered as discovery choices. A removed provider category falls back to All channels with a message. Home's existing category row follows the same discovery view, including its session filters, and its All action opens exactly that view. While browsing Favourites or Recently watched it uses your last remembered topic, provider category or custom list. Adults never appear on Home.

Under each channel's name, a smaller line tells it apart: the subscription it's from when it reads like a channel of another, then its country and qualities. A name or line that doesn't fit shows in full when you point at it, or choose its row with the keys while its schedule is closed. When a subscription's channels couldn't be fetched, a line above the list says so, with **Retry**, and the others show as usual. The channels on the right show what's on now, with how long it has left, and what's next. The channel you're watching plays muted at the top.

- Click a channel to watch it. **Back**, or Escape, returns to the same place in the list.
- The arrow at the end of a row lists the rest of the day. Click a later programme to read about it.
- On a channel your provider keeps recordings of (catch-up), the rest of the day starts with today's programmes that already aired. Click one to watch it from its start. Watch says when it aired, and at its end the channel stops; **Watch** then plays the channel live. A programme older than your provider keeps says how many days back catch-up goes. Catch-up plays on this computer, not on a TV.
- The star adds a channel to your favourites. A new favourite goes last, and **Reorder** puts them in the order you want.

**Guide**, beside **Watch** at the top, or G, shows the same channels as a grid: channels down the side and time across, with a line at now. It goes ahead as far as your provider's guide reaches, and back a couple of hours, or as many days as the longest catch-up among the list's channels keeps, at most a week; hatched stretches are where the guide has nothing. Left and Right move between programmes, Up and Down between channels at the same time, and Page Up, Page Down, Home and End jump as in the list. Click a programme, or move to it, to see what it is under its channel. **Watch live**, Enter or a double click watches the channel; click a channel's name to watch it. Channels with catch-up have a clock sign beside their name. A programme that aired and that catch-up keeps plays from its start with Enter, a double click or **Play from start**, and one on now has **Play from start** after **Watch live**. Programmes that aired and can't be played again are dimmed. Wheels and trackpads scroll both ways, and **Now** comes back to now. **List**, G again or Escape goes back to the list, and Live TV opens in the view you left it in. A search shows each channel it finds once, as the list's shared rows do.

The field beside the list's name searches the list you're looking at: Favourites, Recently watched, All channels, one of your lists, a topic or one provider category. It finds a channel by its name, also the full name your provider gives it, and by the programmes on now and later today, whatever the capitals and accents. The channels it finds stay in the list's order, with what matched underlined. A later programme that matched stands at the end of its row with its time, and the arrow opens the rest of the day on it. Clicking the row still watches the channel as it is now.

In search results, copies that the catalogue can safely identify as one channel share a row. The count says how many channels and playable streams match. **Show copies**, or Right, lists each provider's actual copy with its subscription, qualities and category. Individual rows also name their subscription when several are saved. Enter on the shared row prefers a favourite copy, then the saved subscription order. Enter on a copy plays that copy. Left closes its open schedule first, then the copies. Stars and S apply to individual copies.

/ puts the cursor in the field. Down or Enter there moves to the channels found, and Escape clears the search, as picking another list does. When nothing in the list matches, **Search all channels** looks for the same in every channel. Searching uses what Mr. Streamer already has, so typing asks your provider nothing.

**Reorder**, beside the field in Favourites, or R, puts your favourites in another order. Every row gets an Up and a Down button: a click moves the channel one place, and a click with Shift moves it to the top or the bottom. On the keyboard, Up and Down choose a row, and with Alt held, Option on a Mac, they move it. Page Up and Page Down move it ten places, Home and End to the top and the bottom. **Save**, or Enter, keeps the order. **Cancel**, or Escape, drops it, and so does leaving the list. Nothing plays while you reorder.

A search hides favourites, so Reorder asks you to clear it first and clears nothing by itself. If the order can't be saved, Mr. Streamer says so and keeps what you arranged: **Retry** saves it again. If your favourites changed in the meantime, **Reload** starts over from them.

Home, the guide and the channel list while watching show your favourites in that order, and channel up and down follow it. The channel numbers stay your provider's, and so does the quality each channel plays in. A favourite that isn't showing, a channel for adults while those are hidden or one your provider stopped listing, keeps its place and comes back to it. One quality of a channel that your provider stops listing keeps its place too: if you moved the channel further down meanwhile, it goes back up to that place when the quality returns.

Favourites and watch history from all your subscriptions show as one list each. Each entry still belongs to its own subscription: removing a subscription takes its favourites and history out of the lists, and they come back when you add the same account again, unless you chose to delete them.

Providers often list a channel once per quality: "VRT 1 FHD", "VRT 1 HD", "VRT 1 SD". Mr. Streamer shows those as one channel, with its qualities under the name, when they are surely the same channel: same name, same region and language, and the same category or one named only for a quality, such as "BE | 4K". Anything less certain stays a row of its own. A channel you starred, or watched, under one of its qualities is the same favourite and the same history entry.

Channels your provider marks for adults, or files in a category named for adults, stay hidden until you turn on **For adults** in Settings > Movies and series. Then they show in Live TV's lists and the guide, where the field beside a list's name finds them, but never on Home or in ⌘K's search.

Programme information comes from your provider. Many providers only cover some channels, and some cover none; those channels show their name and category instead. Some providers file unrelated channels under one channel's guide; Mr. Streamer leaves those channels without programmes rather than show the wrong ones. The guide updates every six hours. To add guides from other addresses, or to give a channel the guide channel you pick, see [Guides from other addresses](subscriptions.md#guides-from-other-addresses).

### Your lists

**New list**, after your lists below All channels, makes a list of your own: type its name and press Enter, and it opens, empty. Escape leaves it. A row's list button, beside its star, shows your lists: tick one to put the channel at its end, clear it to take the channel out, or choose **New list** to make one that holds the channel. A list holds channels of any of your subscriptions, in your order, and **Reorder** works as in Favourites. Its **…** button renames it, moves it up or down among your lists, or deletes it after asking once. Two lists can't share a name, whatever the capitals and accents.

The guide, the channel list while watching and Home's category row show the list you picked, in its order, and channel up and down follow it. A channel your provider stops listing stays at the end, dimmed, with **Remove**; if your provider lists it again, even under another of its qualities, it's back in its place. Channels of a subscription you removed are counted under the filters and come back when you add the same account again, unless you ticked **Also delete**, which takes them out of every list. Your lists themselves stay. Channels for adults neither show nor count while those are hidden. If a change can't be saved, Mr. Streamer says so, with **Retry**.

## Watching

While watching, the channel list opens over the left of the picture with the list button, Enter or the left arrow. Click a channel to switch; its title switches to another category, with the same countries and **Find** as Live TV. Clicking the picture shows the controls, and a double click toggles full screen. Scroll over the picture to switch channel, once per gesture.

The bar keeps Channels, Favourite, Stop or Watch, CC when available, volume, full screen and **More**. More holds channel up, channel down, the previous channel, Sound, Quality, Play on and Mini player.

- **Sound**, in More, shows when a channel has more than one sound track. Picking another starts the channel again with it, which takes a moment. An HLS channel, as most of a playlist's are, switches without starting again.
- **CC** lists the channel's subtitles: DVB subtitles, teletext subtitle pages and closed captions, and on an HLS channel the subtitles its stream offers. C turns the last ones you picked on and off.
- **CC** also has **Timing**, which moves teletext subtitles and captions earlier or later until you switch channel, and the rows that set how subtitles look, as for [movies and series](movies-and-series.md#watching). Live channels play at their own speed.
- **Quality**, in More, shows on a channel with several qualities. Its page says which one plays. Q opens it directly.
- **Mini player**, in More, or P, shrinks the window into a small picture on top of other windows, as for [movies and series](movies-and-series.md#watching). Up and Down still switch channel; opening the list puts the window back.
- The keyboard's media keys and the system's own controls show the programme, the channel and its logo. Pause or stop there stops the channel, and **Watch** starts it again. Next and previous do nothing, so a tap on your headphones never changes channel. On [a TV](playback.md#playing-on-a-tv), a channel paused with the TV's own remote shows as paused, and play there plays it on.

The languages you pick carry over to other channels, and to movies and series. A channel in another language starts with subtitles in yours when it has them. Subtitles a stream marks as its own default don't come on by themselves.

Live CC appears once subtitle data arrives. A teletext page that sends only filler or an empty DVB page stays hidden. An HLS channel needs at least one subtitle cue before CC appears, then lists all its declared text languages. Some may be empty; a chosen rendition has a short recovery allowance before turning off if it still cannot load. The picture keeps playing. If that channel reconnects, your chosen language is tried again unless you chose Off. C does nothing while CC is hidden. Proven tracks stay available until you change channel. Your saved language or Off choice applies when that language is proven. After more than ten seconds of playback, late availability flashes "Subtitles available · C" once in Watch if subtitles stay off. HLS discovery checks a few subtitle segments at a time while CC is off, without opening another channel connection. Receiver playback keeps the declared tracks and the reminder that live subtitles play on this computer only.

### Quality

Channels start in Full HD, or the nearest quality the channel has, lower first. Change that in Settings > Live TV. If your provider has no stream for a quality right now, or doesn't answer, Automatic tries the next, at most three, one after another, and the line under the programme says so: "Full HD didn't start, playing HD". If the provider refuses the stream, Automatic stops there instead of trying more streams against a refusal. The status doesn't say why, so Mr. Streamer can't tell whether another quality would play.

Pick a quality in the menu and that channel keeps it. When it doesn't start, Mr. Streamer says why and offers another instead of switching on its own. **Use Automatic** in the menu, or **Reset** in Settings for every channel, goes back to Automatic.

After a channel fell back or failed, the menu says what became of each quality on that try: "No stream · 404", "Refused · 403", "Error · 503", "No data", "No picture", "Can't play", "Playing" or "Not tried". The number is the provider's HTTP status.

The quality is the word your provider puts in the channel's name. The resolution beside it is what the picture actually is. A channel whose name gives no quality says "Not labelled".

### When a channel doesn't play

The middle of the picture says what went wrong, with a small line of what Mr. Streamer observed: the provider's HTTP status, the qualities it tried, how often it reconnected and the time. [What plays](playback.md#when-something-wont-play) lists each message.

- **Retry**, or R, opens the same channel again in the quality it had.
- **Quality** opens the quality menu. Nothing starts until you pick one.
- **Channels** opens the list. **Next channel** takes Quality's place when the channel has no quality left to try.
- A refusal offers Retry and Channels only, and Mr. Streamer never changes channel by itself.
- The cross at the message's top right closes it. The channel stays stopped; **Watch** in the controls, or R, tries it again.

When the picture stands still for three seconds, "Waiting for data" shows at the top right until it moves again. When data stays away for fifteen seconds, or the provider ends the stream, the channel reconnects: up to four times, after 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds, and **Stop** ends that. A channel that comes back and breaks off again within 30 seconds gets no extra tries, so a stream that keeps dropping ends with "Keeps dropping" instead of reconnecting for ever. Once it played for 30 seconds, the next break has all four again. The mini player, and the bar at the foot of the pages while [a TV](playback.md#playing-on-a-tv) plays, say the same in a few words.

## Search

⌘K (Ctrl K on Windows and Linux) searches every channel, movies and series, and the programmes on now and later today, in every subscription. Pick a channel, or a programme that's on, to watch it. Pick a later programme to read about it, or a movie or series to see its details. Opened from Live TV, it starts with what the list's field searched for. Live TV searches the list it shows from [that field](#the-guide), and Movies and Series their own titles from a field in their tabs; see [Movies and series](movies-and-series.md#finding-something). Channel name results use the same safe groups and **Show copies**. Right expands and Left collapses while the search field keeps focus. Programme results keep their own rows after movies and series.

## Reminders

A programme still to come has **Remind me** in its details: under its channel in the guide grid, under its description in the rest of a day, and in ⌘K's search. Once set it reads **Reminder set**, and the programme shows a bell. Press it again to cancel. The bell beside the list's name in Live TV counts the reminders still to come and lists them, with when and on which channel each is. The cross beside one takes it out. Tab reaches the buttons, and Enter presses them.

When the programme starts, Mr. Streamer shows a notice at the top of its window, on any page, with **Watch** and **Dismiss**, and asks your system to show a notification with the programme and its channel. Nothing plays until you press Watch. Mr. Streamer has to be open to remind you: it doesn't wake or start your computer. After the computer slept or the app was closed, a programme that's still on reminds you once, saying how long ago it started; one that already ended is listed as Missed. The in-app notice does not depend on system notifications. System delivery is not guaranteed, and the app cannot tell whether you disabled notifications. If the system reports a delivery failure, the list says so. What a locked screen shows is your system's setting. Clicking a notification brings Mr. Streamer's window forward with its notice.

A reminder belongs to its subscription, channel and start time. If the guide later lists another programme then, the reminder says what it was and reminds you of the new one. If it lists nothing then, or your provider stops listing the channel, it says it's not in the guide now and doesn't remind you, unless the programme comes back. It never moves to a programme of the same name at another time. Reminders are kept in a local `reminders.json` file and cleared when the app checks after the end of the day their programme ends. Removing a subscription removes its reminders. Reminders of adult channels show only in the app while **For adults** is on. A reminder due while they are hidden becomes Missed and does not notify when you show them again.
